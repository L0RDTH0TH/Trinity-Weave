# Structures gen — cultural-historical built environment

**Job:** structures gen is the **societal subsection of worldgen**. It produces the marks people leave on the world — settlements, keeps, ruins, tombs, holds, camps — so that the built environment feels coherent, historical, and culturally legible. Natural terrain is the other half; both must agree.

## Historical causality

No one builds a keep without reason, resources, and people. Ruins are not random; they are the residue of prior occupation, conflict, or abandonment. Every significant structure carries historical meaning (ancient elven city, tomb of a forgotten hero, fallen border keep, etc.).

## Cultural expression (show, don’t tell)

Each race/subculture possesses distinctive settlement and structure patterns that express history, tradition, and way of life through architecture, layout, materials, defensive logic, and spatial habits.

## First-class examples (directional anchors)

| Culture | Texture |
|---------|---------|
| **Elven** | Nature-integrated, elegant curves, living or grown architecture, light and vertical emphasis. |
| **Dwarven** | Subterranean or deeply stone-carved holds, defensive depth, gilded or finely worked stone, endurance aesthetic. |
| **Goliath** (and similar highland peoples) | Mobile or semi-mobile mountain occupation; settlements that can be moved or seasonally re-occupied rather than static cities. |
| **Human** (and analogous adaptable peoples) | Variable by subculture — frontier timber, fortified river towns, dense trade cities, etc. — still required to be internally coherent and historically grounded. |

Additional dialects (other playable or major NPC cultures) follow the same rule: distinctive, legible, lore-tied.

## Active vs residual

| Mode | Texture |
|------|---------|
| **Active settlements** | Living populations, current social/economic/defensive logic, daily rhythm. |
| **Residual structures** | Ruins, tombs, abandoned holds, lost cities — historical meaning, possible lingering threats or recoverable knowledge/treasure, ecological reclamation, residual claims. |

## Lore, faction, and institutional binding

Structures inherit and express cultural, faction, pantheon, and power relationships already present in the world. A temple belongs to a faith; a ruined keep may still carry claims; a thriving town has weight the living world already knows how to treat.

## Player-made structures

When the party renovates, expands, or founds a holding, the same cultural-expression and causality rules apply. Players have **approximation rights**: a character with relevant skill may not know an exact historical technique but can approximate toward a desired expression. Resulting bases should feel unique to the party while remaining inside the world’s architectural grammar. Links to hirelings/staffing demand already locked.

## Ruin density & age

Variable by DM discretion, sub-region, and history generation. No fixed global density.

## Worldgen relationship

Structures gen is downstream of and coherent with broader worldgen, history, and cultural distribution. It consumes the same seed and cultural map; it does not invent isolated content that contradicts them.

## Future compatibility

Cultural dialects and historical logic are treated as consumable data. The pattern remains compatible with a future portable “lore package” system that would let tables share the same cultural-historical grammar across different geographies.

## Player readability

Characters with appropriate knowledge, background, or observation can read cultural and historical signals from the built environment.

## Refuse (required)

- Random dungeon placement with no causal history
- Generic fantasy-town or generic-dungeon skins applied to every people
- Ruins as empty combat containers only
- Structures that ignore the living institutional, cultural, and faction map
- Parallel generator that can contradict the rest of the world
- Culture that exists only in exposition text rather than in form
